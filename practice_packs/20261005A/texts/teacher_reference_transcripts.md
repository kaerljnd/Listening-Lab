You've probably used many remote controls for things like televisions, smart devices, and other electronics. My wife uses one to open the gate to her parking lot at work now. Remote controls for TVs have been around since the 1950s. While remote controls had been used before then, back to around 1870, they were mostly for unpiloted military vehicles. Later, the inventor Nikola Tesla developed a wireless remote control for military vehicles in 1898.

As for TV remotes, the first really popular one was developed in 1950. It was called the Lazy Bones and was pretty expensive. Unfortunately, the Lazy Bones was connected to the TV by a long wire, which was annoying and a trip hazard. It's kind of hard to imagine this was more practical for most people than just getting up and turning the dial on the television yourself.

A few years later, in 1955, a wireless remote was introduced: the Flash-Matic. To use the Flash-Matic, you'd point it at one of the corners of your TV screen. This would shine a light beam at the chosen corner. Now, each corner had its own purpose: channel up or down, power, or mute. Each corner had a photoelectric cell to convert the light beam into an electrical signal to do what you want. Trouble was, the photoelectric cells responded to other light sources too, like from a light bulb or even sunlight. Imagine turning on a light bulb and your TV turns on too.

Let's move on in our renewable energy series to discuss solar power, which is arguably the most abundant resource available. Solar power takes energy from the sun's rays and converts it to electricity or heat. The primary technology for this is solar panels made up of photovoltaic cells, which generate an electric current when sunlight hits them.

The advantages of solar power are well known, and the most important may be the fact that it produces zero harmful pollutants. Any industrial endeavor will produce carbon dioxide in one way or another, and manufacturing solar panels itself requires energy sourced from fossil fuels. But on the whole, the industry has a much lower carbon footprint than coal or natural gas and is a key to reducing greenhouse gas emissions.

Another advantage, and one we don't talk about enough, is that solar power can provide energy access in remote and underserved areas, far from the grid.

What are the disadvantages? Solar panel efficiency is affected by cloud cover and other weather, which reduces the amount of sunlight reaching the panels. That said, innovations in battery storage systems do allow for the storage of solar energy for use during non-sunny periods. Panel lifespan is long, but improvements in efficiency are paradoxically driving panels to be replaced much earlier than that. So waste is increasing faster than expected. This is a problem because recycling them is expensive. Most panels end up in landfills.

The Renaissance was a pivotal period in European history spanning roughly the fourteenth to the seventeenth centuries. The Renaissance is characterized by a renewed interest in art, science, and literature. It marked a significant departure from the Middle Ages and laid the groundwork for the modern era.

One of the defining features of the Renaissance was the revival of classical learning. Scholars and artists looked back to the texts and artistic achievements of ancient Greece and Rome for inspiration. This led to a flourishing of ideas and creativity, evident in the works of figures like Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, and Galileo.

The invention of the printing press by Johannes Gutenberg around 1440 was a crucial development during this time. It allowed for the mass production of books, making literature and scholarly works more accessible to a broader audience. This contributed to increased literacy and the spread of Renaissance ideas.

The Renaissance also saw major advancements, particularly in astronomy. Nicolaus Copernicus proposed a heliocentric model of the solar system, placing the sun, not the Earth, at the center. Galileo Galilei later used a telescope to observe celestial bodies, providing evidence that supported Copernicus's theory. These discoveries challenged long-held beliefs and laid the foundation for modern astronomy.

Despite political instability in some regions, the Renaissance left a lasting legacy on European society. Its influence can still be seen today in art, science, and education.

Today, let's talk about the ancient Maya civilization and explore a fascinating revelation about its decline—a vast civilization with impressive cities and a flourishing culture that suddenly crumbled into history.

For years, scholars have debated what might have happened. And now, recent research has brought stalagmites into the spotlight. Stalagmites, those formations rising up from the floor of a cave, are more than just geological wonders. They serve as natural archives, capturing changes in precipitation over thousands of years.

In 2005, scientists analyzed stalagmites from caves near the former Maya heartland. The data revealed a series of prolonged droughts that coincided with periods of decline for Maya society. The Maya were heavily dependent on agriculture, with water being a critical resource for their survival. When these droughts occurred, the scarcity of water put pressure on their food production and societal structures. Imagine cities struggling without enough water for crops, leading to food shortages and social unrest.

It's important to note, though, that while these climate changes were significant, they weren't the sole cause. Other factors like warfare and politics played a role too. Yet the revelation from these stalagmites provides a clearer picture of how environmental changes can trigger societal shifts. This discovery not only sheds light on the past but also serves as a reminder of our vulnerability to climate change today.
